In 2009, I lived in Guimaraes, Portugal, and worked at the Center for Computer Graphics developing applications for multi-touch interfaces. For the interactive tunnel at the Guimaraes Science Center, I developed an application that let visitors explore the circulatory system through multi-touch interactions on the projection surface.

The installation also featured software about the Solar System, developed by someone else. The journey began at the Sun and advanced automatically from planet to planet, passing Mercury, the asteroid belt, and ending at Neptune. At each stop, there were information points that visitors could explore through multi-touch.

The curved, tunnel-shaped projection showed the Solar System from two opposing viewpoints, making the experience more intense and immersive.

The immersive tunnel included:

- a six-meter curved structure with plexiglass and a projection screen;
- six projectors on each side of the tunnel;
- infrared emitter bars;
- cameras with visible-light filters;
- two computers controlling projection and cameras;
- one computer running the application;
- a router;
- an amplifier and four speakers.

**Partners:** Ciencia Viva - National Agency for Scientific and Technological Culture, Guimaraes City Council, and the University of Minho.

Today, this kind of technology is well established, but at the time it was a new challenge: we were trying to create open-source reproductions of interfaces similar to Microsoft Surface. One unusual fact from that period is that I took part in the [founding of Wikimedia Portugal](https://wikimedia.pt/Reuniões/Primeira_Assembleia-Geral).
